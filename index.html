What these maps show

The annual tidal maps depict the number of UTC calendar days during which a 2 m terrain pixel exceeds 5 cm modeled water depth, conditioned on the corresponding 20 m SFINCS cell being hydraulically wet. A day counts if at least one hourly snapshot meets that threshold.

The 2050 and 2100 experiments apply JPL relative sea-level increments of +0.197504 m and +0.643954 m to the same 2024 tidal forcing. Geometry and 2021 C-CAP land-cover classification are held fixed. These are controlled sea-level sensitivity experiments, not predictions of future weather or storm frequency.

The flood-day difference maps subtract the 2024 annual counts from the future-scenario counts. Negative differences, if present, are shown separately. The 348-day display exclusion is a visualization filter, not a hydrodynamic correction; retained water and connectivity require further QA.

The map uses 10 m approximate-resolution image previews for web performance. Scientific analysis uses the accompanying full-resolution 2 m GeoTIFFs. Map click values are sampled from the original 2 m scientific raster; preview positioning on a web tile basemap is approximate; use the original geo-referenced TIFFs for accurate location-by-location scientific analysis.

Storm flooding: Historical Sandy replay is available where the existing map is installed. The 1-in-10-year and 1-in-100-year events and future Sandy replays have not been simulated. They require separately justified extreme-water-level/tide/storm forcing and event simulations. A 100-year return period applies to a specified baseline distribution, not automatically to its sea-level-shifted future counterpart.

Methods: SFINCS 20 m hydraulics; original 2 m topobathymetry; C-CAP classes 2/3/4 considered developed. The regional sea-level forcing and model assumptions should be documented in a full technical methods note before public release.

Future Sea Level: Ocean-connected below-mean-water inundation is estimated from fixed 2 m terrain using an explicitly specified 2024 MHHW elevation in NAVD88. This static calculation does not simulate tides, drainage, defenses, waves, or geomorphic evolution. Sea-level offsets are from JPL RCP8.5 member 01, not an ensemble mean. The shoreline position is indicative, not a forecast.

Reconstructed tidal inundation frequency

The annual tidal flooding maps use hourly SFINCS hydrodynamic simulations with 2024 tidal forcing under 2024, 2050, and 2100 relative sea-level conditions. Flood-day frequencies are evaluated using 2 m terrain elevations and a 5 cm inundation-depth threshold.

In some locations, the hydrodynamic model retains water after an inundation event, potentially because local drainage pathways are not explicitly resolved. For cells identified as persistently nonresponsive to tides, we estimate tidal inundation frequency using nearby tidally responsive model water levels, the local subgrid wetting threshold, and the underlying 2 m terrain.

These reconstructed results estimate days of potential tidal inundation, rather than days that standing water necessarily remains on the ground. The reconstruction does not independently establish hydraulic connectivity or explicitly simulate stormwater drainage.

The method is experimental. Results are not observations of street or property flooding and should not be used for emergency planning, engineering design, insurance decisions, or property-level flood-risk determinations.

Future scenarios apply relative sea-level changes to the same 2024 tidal forcing. They are controlled sea-level sensitivity experiments rather than predictions of future daily weather or storm events.
